Overview

A food aggregator’s demand map

FoodHub connects customers to restaurants through one app. Prep happens in-kitchen; couriers confirm pickup and drop-off; customers may rate. The business earns a fixed margin on each order.

Role. As data scientist, I analyzed registered-customer order history to quantify restaurant demand, weekday vs weekend behavior, time-to-door, rating coverage, and commission revenue under the stated fee rules.

Why it matters. Without a clear picture of volume concentration, rating gaps, and SLA outliers, promotions and staffing stay guesswork. This project answers those questions with transparent pandas workflows — not black-box models.

EDA

Used shape, info(), and null audits to confirm a complete 1,898×9 frame. Called out that casting high-cardinality object columns to category would shrink memory if the table grew — engineering judgment beyond the homework checklist.

EDA

“Not given” is stored as a string, not NaN. Treating it as a category preserves signal about feedback coverage (736 orders / ~38.7%). Numeric rating averages were computed only after filtering non-numeric values — avoiding silent coercion bugs.

Visualization

Histograms and boxplots show cost slightly right-skewed with most tickets in the $10–$20 band; prep tightly ranged 20–35 minutes (mean ≈ median ≈ 27). Countplots surface American / Shake Shack concentration and weekend dominance (~71%).

Ops

Boxplots by day_of_the_week show weekend mean delivery just over ~22.5 minutes vs weekday more than five minutes slower — a staffing / traffic lever, not a cuisine effect. Weekend IQR is wider under higher volume.

Ops

Derived total time as prep + delivery. 10.54% of orders (200) exceed 60 minutes — a concrete outlier set for restaurant and route review rather than a vague “slow deliveries” complaint.

Business logic

Grouped by restaurant, aggregated rating count and mean, then filtered rating_count > 50 and avg_rating > 4. Qualifiers: Shake Shack, The Meatball Shop, Blue Ribbon Sushi, Blue Ribbon Fried Chicken — a transparent partnership shortlist.

Business logic

Applied fee rules vectorized with boolean masks: 25% when cost > $20, 15% when cost > $5 and ≤ $20. Net platform surcharge revenue: $6,166.30. Orders ≤ $5 contribute $0 under the stated policy — important for upsell design.

EDA

Heatmap across cost, prep, and delivery shows no strong linear relationships. Scatter views likewise fail to show delivery time alone driving ratings — a useful check against overfitted “faster = happier” narratives without richer features.
